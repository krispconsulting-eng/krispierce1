# Rare Intelligence: Brand

The course brand for rare disease capability. Its own identity, complementary to
Kris Pierce Consulting but not derived from it.

## The idea

Rare Intelligence teaches people the systems that decide rare disease outcomes,
and gives them the working knowledge to act inside those systems. "Rare" names
the field. "Intelligence" names what the courses give: the insight and capability
to move, not just awareness.

Where Kris Pierce Consulting is a senior practice speaking peer to peer, Rare
Intelligence is a learning product speaking to learners: clear, confident, warm,
and a little more energetic. It is allowed to be engaging in a way a consulting
practice is not, because its job is to make people want to learn.

## Relationship to Kris Pierce Consulting

Complementary, not subordinate. Rare Intelligence is developed in partnership
with Kris Pierce Consulting, and the credibility of the practice sits behind it,
but it carries its own name and identity everywhere. The connection is stated as
an endorsement line ("in partnership with Kris Pierce Consulting"), never by
borrowing the practice's colours, fonts or logo.

The two should look good side by side: same seriousness, same clarity, different
temperament. Kris is calm, warm and editorial. Rare Intelligence is cooler,
brighter and more product-like.

## Voice

- **To learners, not peers.** Clear and encouraging without being a cheerleader.
  It explains, it does not hype.
- **Outcome-first.** Lead with what a learner will be able to do.
- **Plain and confident.** No jargon for its own sake, no manufactured urgency,
  no countdown-timer marketing.
- **Australian English**, and no em dashes.

## Colour

An ocean-blue system, chosen to read as knowledgeable and dependable, with a
teal partner for freshness and a coral spark for energy. Full values are in
`design-system/tokens.css` and `tokens.json`.

- **Ocean** (primary): `#2E6FB2`, deepening to `#1D4A7C` and `#10314F`. Buttons,
  primary accents, the pathway gradient, deep surfaces.
- **Teal** (secondary): `#16B0A3`. The free tier, currency cues, the second
  gradient stop, a fresher accent.
- **Coral** (spark): `#FF6F4D`. Used sparingly, for energy only: the whole-pack
  highlight, a single lively accent. Never the dominant colour.
- **Neutrals:** a cool, navy-based set, page background `#F4F8FC`, paper white,
  deep navy ink `#0F2438`. Deliberately not warm cream, so it reads as its own
  brand next to Kris.

Tiers are colour-coded across the three accents (teal, ocean, coral) so a learner
can feel the shape of a program at a glance.

## Type

- **Display: Bricolage Grotesque.** Characterful, contemporary, a little warm. It
  gives headlines personality without shouting, and it is clearly not Outfit.
- **Body: Figtree.** Clean, friendly, highly readable at UI sizes. Clearly not
  Archivo.

Both are embedded in `design-system/fonts.css`, so the brand carries its own
type with no network dependency. The type scale is in the tokens.

## The motif: the pathway

Learning is a pathway, and the courses are built in tiers, so the brand's
signature device is a **node-and-line pathway**: numbered nodes joined by a line,
seen in the hero pathway card, as the leading node on every eyebrow label, and in
the brand mark. It is drawn in plain CSS and simple SVG, never a heavy asset.

## The mark

The wordmark is "Rare Intelligence" set in Bricolage Grotesque, "Rare" in medium
and "Intelligence" in bold ocean. It is preceded by a small **signal glyph**: a
filled node with a ring, the pathway motif compressed to a single point, standing
for a signal of intelligence. The wordmark can ship as live text; a drawn logo
can follow but is not needed to launch.

## What not to do

- Do not reintroduce the Kris Pierce palette, fonts or logo. The brands are
  meant to look different.
- Do not let coral become a main colour. It is a spark, not a base.
- Do not add a third typeface, a new palette, or motivational-marketing language.
- No em dashes, anywhere.
