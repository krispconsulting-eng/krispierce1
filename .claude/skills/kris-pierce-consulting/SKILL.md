# Kris Pierce Consulting: Master Brand Voice

This is the source of truth for how Kris sounds. Every other consulting skill defers to it. If anything here conflicts with another skill, this wins. The voice is the product; protect it. This guide tracks the live site at krispierce.com.au; when the site moves, this moves with it.

## The positioning spine

Everything traces back to one line:

> Kris Pierce is a strategic advisor in consumer engagement, rare disease, and health policy, working with health organisations, industry partners, and not-for-profits to generate insights and reach patient-centred outcomes.

The official hero line, used verbatim where the brand line is wanted:

> Insights. Patient-centred outcomes. Flexible methodology. Together, we discover what works.

If a piece of content cannot be traced back to the spine, it is off-brand, however well written.

These phrases recur across the site and should recur in content. They are the brand's vocabulary, not filler.

## The five core messages

These are the things every piece of content should reinforce, one at a time. Pick the one the piece is making; do not try to make all five at once.

1. **Real partnership, not tokenism.** Kris does genuine participatory work with patients, carers, and community. She is not a "giving consumers a voice" consultant. She will name the difference between real co-design and the box-ticking version plainly. Engagement that looks good in a report but changes nothing is not the goal; the work has to shift a decision, a design, or a strategy.
2. **Lived experience and professional rigour at the same table.** More than two decades as a carer in rare disease, brought into the work rather than kept separate. It informs judgement; it is context, not the pitch, and never a sympathy story.
3. **She understands the systems decisions move through.** Senior national and international advisory roles (see Credentials) mean she knows how decisions actually get made, not just how engagement is talked about.
4. **Insight generation that pays off.** Flexible methodology that produces insight organisations can act on. The method follows the question. The point is never the method; it is what changes because of it.
5. **Patient-centred outcomes are the point.** The work exists to move outcomes toward the people most affected, not to perform consultation.

## The audiences and their angle

Three audiences. Each cares about a different payoff. Match the angle to the reader. Lead with the audience and the work, not with Kris.

- **Health organisations and research teams:** participatory research strengthens outcomes. The angle is better evidence and better decisions, engagement built into the work rather than bolted on at the end. They come when patient voice needs to be more than a line in a grant application.
- **Industry partners (pharmaceutical, MedTech):** authentic patient and carer insight reduces risk and builds trust, before, during, and after launch. They come when preparing for launch, navigating reimbursement, or bringing patient experience into evidence and strategy. The Medicines Australia Code of Conduct shapes this work: she works inside it, not around it. Keep this content general and never imply endorsement of a named product or sponsor; when a piece drifts toward a specific medicine or sponsor, flag it for Kris rather than publishing.
- **Not-for-profits and advocacy groups:** real partnership with community strengthens mission. The angle is engagement that carries real weight with members, funders, and the bodies that make decisions about their community.

## Language markers (how she sounds)

- **Lead with you, the work, or the organisation; reserve "I" for emphasis.** This is the "option 4" rule the live site follows. Most sentences and openings should start with the reader, the work, or the audience, not "I". Count the "I"-starts and cut them back; keep only the ones that genuinely earn the first person. Use "you", "we", and "together" to signal partnership.
- **Peer to peer.** She is an expert talking to other senior people, not a coach, trainer, or motivational speaker.
- **Plain and direct.** Concrete before abstract. Jargon only when it is the precise word, never to sound credible.
- **Willing to commit to a view.** She holds positions and defends them with evidence. Neutral summaries are not her voice.
- **Measured, occasionally dry.** Confident without hype. Dry humour is fine. Performed enthusiasm and urgency are not. Let the track record and the work sell, not adjectives.
- **Australian and British English.** Colour, organisation, centre, programme, recognise.
- **Sentence case** everywhere except the tracked, uppercase overline labels.
- **One idea at a time.** She actively avoids the scattered, everything-at-once style. Hold one thought and make it well.
- **Numerals stay modest and concrete** ("20+ years", "two decades"), never inflated growth-hack stats.

## What she is not

A cheerleader. A trainer. A motivational speaker. A "voice for the voiceless" consultant. An inspirational-quote account. Anyone who confuses being consulted with being in partnership. No "revolutionary, world-class, 10x" language. No "don't miss out, limited spots" urgency. If a draft drifts into any of these, rewrite it.

## Banned language

- **No corporate jargon:** stakeholder, leverage, best practice, synergy, and the like.
- **No casual closings:** cheers, happy to help, no worries.
- **No emoji** in brand copy. The brand mark is the script wordmark, used on its own.
- **No exclamation marks.** No ALL-CAPS shouting.
- **No em dashes.** Use commas, colons, semicolons, brackets, or full stops.

## Pre-publishing checklist

Run this on every piece before it goes out. Other skills explicitly call back to this list.

1. **Traceable to the spine?** Can this be tied back to the one-line positioning? If not, fix or drop it.
2. **One idea?** Exactly one. If a second is fighting to get in, it is the next piece.
3. **Substance present?** Does it teach, argue, or show something? No empty self-promotion.
4. **"I"-restraint applied?** Count the "I"-starts. Lead with you, the work, or the organisation; keep first person only where it earns its place.
5. **Right register?** Peer to peer, not coach. No motivational, inspirational, or urgency tone.
6. **Lived experience handled right?** Context that grounds a point, never currency, never sympathy bait, never the headline of a sales piece.
7. **Credentials accurate?** Real titles from the Credentials list, not vague summaries or inflated claims.
8. **Code-safe?** No named products or sponsors in public-facing or pharma-adjacent content. When in doubt, generalise and flag for Kris.
9. **Banned language clear?** No corporate jargon, no casual closings, no emoji, no exclamation marks, no ALL-CAPS.
10. **Australian and British English?** Spelling and idiom.
11. **Em dash check.** No em dashes. Anywhere. Check every time; this one is non-negotiable.

If a piece is going live and the stakes are real, offer a vibe-check (the vibe-check skill) as the final honest read.
