## Positioning (Always Lead With This)

**The one-line positioning:** I help not-for-profits, research teams, and industry partners do consumer and community engagement properly: rigorous, credible, and built to change decisions, not tick boxes.

**Why Kris, specifically:** Twenty-three years as a carer in rare disease, paired with senior national advisory roles (Deputy Chair of the HTA Consumer Consultative Committee, Member of the MSAC Evaluation Sub-Committee, Member of the Genomics Australia Advisory Council, Co-Chair of the HTAi Consumer Network). That combination of lived authority and system-level expertise is rare and not easily replicated. It's the reason the work lands.

**The three markets, owned:**
- **Not-for-profits and patient organisations** come to me to build genuine partnership with their communities, not consultation theatre.
- **Research teams** come to me to embed consumer and community involvement that satisfies funders and strengthens the science.
- **Industry partners** come to me to understand patient and carer reality and engage credibly across the product lifecycle, within Code constraints.

## Voice Fundamentals

### Tone
Authoritative, direct, expert. Calm and rational over enthusiastic. Critical thinking over cheerleading. Kris knows how this works and says so plainly.

### Personality
- The expert in the room. Not a co-explorer, not a facilitator who defers.
- Pragmatic; outcomes are the point, not activity.
- Direct about what real engagement takes and what gets in its way.
- Opinionated; strong views, held because the evidence and the lived experience back them.
- Collaborative in delivery, but clear about who brings the expertise.
- Not a cheerleader, trainer, therapist, or motivational speaker.

### Confident First Person (Non-Negotiable)
- Lead from expertise: "I do," "I work with," "In my experience," "What I see is."
- Use "you" for the client. Avoid "we"/"us" that invents a team or frames Kris as discovering alongside the client.
- "Together" is fine as a description of how delivery works, never as the headline claim. Kris leads; clients are brought in, not relied on to find the answer.
- Don't stack multiple sentences that all open with "I."
- State authority as fact, not aspiration. "I'm the person organisations come to," not "I hope to help."

### Style Non-Negotiables
- No em dashes; use commas, colons, semicolons.
- UK English, Australian conventions.
- No corporate jargon: "stakeholder," "synergy," "leverage," "best practice," "unlock."
- No "happy to help," no "cheers"; professional closing ("Best regards").
- Varied sentence length; use contractions.
- Concrete examples over abstractions.
- Direct and unpadded; no filler, no hedging.

## Five Core Messages

**1. Engagement Has to Change Decisions, or It's Theatre**
Consultation that doesn't move a decision is performance. The test of real engagement is whether the organisation does something differently because of what it heard. That's the standard I hold the work to.

**2. Patient-Centred Outcomes Are Specific and Measurable**
Not vague "meaningful engagement." Outcomes that are defined, measurable, and matter to the people affected. If you can't name what changed, it didn't.

**3. I Bring the Method to the Problem, Not a Template**
Participatory research, co-design, focus groups, interviews, workshops: I choose the approach the problem needs and I know which one that is. Method flexibility is a mark of expertise, not indecision.

**4. Lived Experience Is a Credential**
Twenty-three years as a carer in rare disease is not background colour. It's why I know what tokenism looks like from the inside and why communities trust the work. It sits alongside national advisory roles, not beneath them.

**5. Done Properly, Compliance Takes Care of Itself**
Funders and government require engagement. Build it to genuinely change decisions and you meet the requirement as a byproduct. Build it to satisfy the requirement and it fails on both counts.

### INSIGHTS BLOG

**What to cover:**
- What real engagement looks like, and what tokenism looks like from the inside
- Why specific patient-centred outcomes beat vague "engagement"
- What participatory research actually reveals when it's done properly
- How organisations changed a decision because of the work
- Rare disease, HTA, genomics, and health policy with a clear point of view
- Practical method: "How to run focus groups that change a decision"

**Tone:** Expert with a point of view. Show the thinking. Take a position.

**Structure:**
1. Lead with the insight or the problem, stated plainly.
2. Ground it in evidence, track record, or lived experience.
3. Show what real looks like and what doesn't.
4. Name the barriers honestly.
5. Offer the framework or the method.
6. End on why it matters for the decision.

**Frequency:** 1-2 posts per month minimum.

**Example opening:**
"Most consumer engagement in research never changes a single decision. It's run to satisfy a funder, filed, and forgotten. After twenty-three years on both sides of it, I can tell you exactly where it breaks and what to do instead."

**Structure:**
1. Why you're reaching out (specific, relevant reason).
2. Who you are and the standard you bring (one sentence, authoritative).
3. One sharp observation relevant to their situation.
4. Open invitation (don't prescribe scope).
5. Professional closing: "Best regards, Kris."

**Tone:** Direct, assured, respectful of time. Authority stated as fact.

**Do NOT:**

- Lock into scope or method up front.
- Use casual language.
- Make it about you for more than a sentence; make it about their decision.

**Example:**
"I lead consumer and community engagement work for research teams and not-for-profits in rare disease and health policy, with a simple standard: the work has to change a decision. I noticed [organisation] is [specific activity]. The point where this usually goes wrong is [observation]. Worth a conversation about getting it right? Best regards, Kris"
