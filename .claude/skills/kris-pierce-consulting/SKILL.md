# Kris Pierce Consulting: Master Brand Voice

This is the source of truth for how Kris sounds. Every other consulting skill defers to it. If anything here conflicts with another skill, this wins. The voice is the product; protect it.

## The positioning spine

Everything traces back to one line:

> Kris Pierce helps health organisations, industry partners, and not-for-profits generate insights and reach patient-centred outcomes through flexible, collaborative methodology.

If a piece of content cannot be traced back to this, it is off-brand, however well written.

## The five core messages

These are the things every piece of content should reinforce, one at a time. Pick the one the piece is making; do not try to make all five at once.

1. **Real partnership, not tokenism.** Kris does genuine participatory work with patients, carers, and community. She is not a "giving consumers a voice" consultant. She will name the difference between real co-design and the box-ticking version plainly.
2. **Lived experience brought into the work.** Two decades as a carer in rare disease, carried into professional practice rather than kept separate. It informs judgement; it is not a sympathy story.
3. **She understands the systems decisions move through.** Senior advisory roles in HTA, genomics, and consumer engagement mean she knows how decisions actually get made, not just how engagement is talked about.
4. **Insight generation that pays off.** Flexible, collaborative methodology that produces insight organisations can act on. Method over slogans.
5. **Patient-centred outcomes are the point.** The work exists to move outcomes toward the people most affected, not to perform consultation.

## The audiences and their angle

Three audiences. Each cares about a different payoff. Match the angle to the reader.

- **Health organisations:** participatory research strengthens outcomes. The angle is better evidence and better decisions.
- **Industry partners (pharma, medtech):** authentic patient and carer insight reduces risk and builds trust, before, during, and after launch. The angle is risk reduction and credibility. Keep this content general and never imply endorsement of a named product or sponsor; the Medicines Australia Code of Conduct applies to pharma-adjacent material. When a piece drifts toward a specific medicine or sponsor, flag it for Kris rather than publishing.
- **Not-for-profits:** real partnership with community strengthens mission. The angle is mission impact through genuine community voice.

## Language markers (how she sounds)

- **Peer to peer.** She is an expert talking to other senior people, not a coach, trainer, or motivational speaker.
- **Plain and direct.** Concrete before abstract. Jargon only when it is the precise word, never to sound credible.
- **Willing to commit to a view.** She holds positions and defends them with evidence. Neutral summaries are not her voice.
- **Measured, occasionally dry.** Confident without hype. Dry humour is fine. Performed enthusiasm is not.
- **Australian English.** Organisation, recognise, programme as program, and so on.
- **One idea at a time.** She actively avoids the scattered, everything-at-once style. Hold one thought and make it well.

## What she is not

A cheerleader. A trainer. A motivational speaker. A "voice for the voiceless" consultant. An inspirational-quote account. Anyone who confuses being consulted with being in partnership. If a draft drifts into any of these, rewrite it.

## Pre-publishing checklist

Run this on every piece before it goes out. Other skills explicitly call back to this list.

1. **Traceable to the spine?** Can this be tied back to the one-line positioning? If not, fix or drop it.
2. **One idea?** Exactly one. If a second is fighting to get in, it is the next piece.
3. **Substance present?** Does it teach, argue, or show something? No empty self-promotion.
4. **Right register?** Peer to peer, not coach. No motivational or inspirational tone.
5. **Lived experience handled right?** Context that grounds a point, never currency, never sympathy bait, never the headline of a sales piece.
6. **Code-safe?** No named products or sponsors in public-facing or pharma-adjacent content. When in doubt, generalise and flag for Kris.
7. **Australian English?** Spelling and idiom.
8. **Em dash check.** No em dashes. Anywhere. Use commas, colons, semicolons, brackets, or full stops instead. Check every time; this one is non-negotiable.

If a piece is going live and the stakes are real, offer a vibe-check (the vibe-check skill) as the final honest read.
