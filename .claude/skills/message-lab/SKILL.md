# Message Lab: Settle the Hero Message First

Don't draft on top of an unsettled idea. When Kris brings raw, tangled, or uncertain material, this skill finds the single message worth making before any drafting starts. Drafting first and hoping the point emerges wastes effort and produces scattered content, which is exactly what Kris wants to avoid.

## When to use it

- Kris has several thoughts and they are competing.
- She knows there is something here but not what to lead with.
- A draft already exists but feels like it is making three points at once.
- Any time the angle is not obvious.

## The process

1. **Dump everything.** Get all the raw thoughts out, unedited. Do not tidy yet.
2. **Find the candidate messages.** Name each distinct idea in the dump as a one-sentence claim. Usually there are two to four hiding in there.
3. **Test each against the spine.** Which candidates trace cleanly back to the positioning spine (see kris-pierce-consulting) and to one of the five core messages? Drop the ones that do not.
4. **Pick the hero.** One survives. The sharpest, most defensible, most positioning-relevant claim. The others are future pieces, not this one.
5. **Sharpen it.** Rewrite the hero as a single, concrete, committed sentence. Not a topic ("co-design"), a claim ("most co-design is consultation wearing a costume").
6. **Pick the pillar and audience.** Which content pillar is it (insight, point of view, how it works, who she helps)? Which audience is it for? That decides the angle.
7. **Hand off.** State the hero message, the pillar, and the audience, then return to the drafting skill. Do not write the final piece here.

## What a good hero message looks like

- One sentence. One claim.
- Concrete, not a topic label.
- Committed: it takes a position someone could disagree with.
- Traceable to the spine and to one core message.
- Owns one idea, leaving the others for later.

## Guardrails

- Resist drafting. The temptation is to keep going into the full piece; stop at the agreed message.
- Kill darlings honestly. If a beloved thought does not trace to the spine, it is not this piece.
- One hero per piece. If two messages both feel essential, they are two pieces.
- Voice still applies even to the sharpened sentence: peer to peer, plain, no motivational register, no em dashes.
