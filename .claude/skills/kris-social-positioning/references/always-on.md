# Always-on positioning (the ongoing engine)

Always-on is the default mode once the launch arc is done. Every post reinforces who Kris is, what she does, and who she helps, even when the surface topic is a sector observation, a research insight, or a policy take. The trick is that positioning is threaded through real content, so the feed never sounds like a rolling advertisement.

## When to use this mode

- Routine, ongoing content.
- Any single post outside a campaign.
- "What should I post this week" requests.

## The core principle

Positioning by demonstration, not declaration. A post about what real co-design looks like positions her as the person who does real co-design, without ever saying "hire me for co-design." Explicit "who I am" posts are the seasoning, not the meal. Most weeks she should not run one at all; the insight and point-of-view posts do the positioning by being good.

## The pillar mix

Draw from the four shared pillars (see SKILL.md). In always-on, the rough balance is:

- **Insight (lead pillar):** the largest share. A specific finding or observation from the work and what it changed.
- **Point of view:** a strong, defensible take on consumer engagement, co-design, HTA, or rare disease policy.
- **How it actually works:** practical method content, useful enough to save. The real version versus the tokenistic version.
- **Who she is / who she helps:** sparingly. A handful of times a quarter, not weekly.

If unsure what to post, default to insight. It is the strongest pillar and the easiest to make positioning-relevant without trying.

## A workable weekly cadence

This is a sustainable baseline, not a quota. Quality beats volume every time.

- **LinkedIn:** one to two posts a week. The priority channel; if effort is scarce, it goes here.
- **X:** two to four short posts a week, reactive and quick. A sharp take on something current, a line pulled from a longer piece.
- **Instagram:** one post a week, a human or visual entry point into the work.
- **Substack:** one piece every two to four weeks, which then seeds the other channels.

A realistic rhythm: write or pick the week's central idea, make the Substack or LinkedIn version, then extract to the faster channels. One good idea can legitimately feed all four.

## Turning anything into a positioning post

When Kris brings a raw input (a thing she noticed, an article, a moment from a project), find the angle that ladders up to the spine:

1. What is the one idea here?
2. Which pillar is it (insight, point of view, how it works)?
3. What does it imply for health organisations, industry, or NFPs? That implication is the quiet positioning.
4. Which platform fits it best, and what is its native shape (see the platform reference files)?

If the idea will not settle, route to the message-lab skill to find the hero message before drafting. Do not draft on top of an unsettled idea.

## Guardrails specific to always-on

- Never let explicit positioning crowd out substance. If a week's posts all say "this is what I do," the engine has failed.
- One idea per post. The scatter is most tempting here, in low-stakes routine content.
- No motivational or inspirational register.
- Lived experience grounds a point occasionally; it is context, not currency, and never sympathy bait.
- Keep industry content general; no named products or sponsors.
- Run the master checklist, including the em dash check, before every post.
