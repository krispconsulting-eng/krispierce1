# Launch / relaunch campaign (the finite introductory arc)

A launch is a finite, sequenced run of posts that introduces or reintroduces Kris Pierce Consulting. It has a beginning, a middle, and an end. Unlike the always-on engine, it deliberately front-loads the "who I am, what I do, who I help" story, because right now the audience genuinely does not know yet. Once the arc is done, hand over to always-on so the feed does not keep sounding like an announcement.

## When to use this mode

- A first public launch of the practice.
- A relaunch or repositioning after a change in focus.
- Any time Kris wants a defined, deliberate run of posts to establish the practice from a standing start.

## The arc

Think in four beats. Each beat can be one post or a small cluster, and each is rendered natively per platform (see the platform reference files). The arc runs primarily on LinkedIn, anchored by a Substack piece, with X and Instagram as native echoes.

1. **The why (the founding idea).** Not "I started a consulting practice." The conviction behind it: what she keeps seeing in health, research, and rare disease that the practice exists to fix. Lead with the problem she is moving toward, not the announcement. This is the Substack anchor piece; LinkedIn takes its sharpest argument.

2. **The what (how she works).** The method, plainly. Flexible, collaborative, real partnership rather than the tokenistic version. This is where she draws the line between what she does and the "giving consumers a voice" cliche she is not. One concrete example of the method beating the box-ticking version.

3. **The who (who she helps and why it pays off).** One post per audience, or one post that covers the three angles cleanly: participatory research strengthens outcomes for health organisations; authentic patient and carer insight reduces risk and builds trust for industry partners; real partnership strengthens mission for NFPs. Keep industry content general and Code-safe.

4. **The proof and the open door.** Credibility as evidence, not a brag list: two decades of lived experience brought into the work, senior roles in HTA, genomics, and consumer engagement, a track record across the sectors. Close the arc with a plain, low-key invitation to work together. This is the one moment a direct CTA is earned.

## Sequencing and cadence

- Run the arc over two to four weeks. Long enough to breathe, short enough to feel like a campaign.
- LinkedIn carries the spine, roughly one beat every few days.
- Substack publishes the founding-idea piece at or just before beat one, so the other channels can point to it.
- X echoes each beat as a sharp standalone or a short thread within a day of the LinkedIn post.
- Instagram gives each beat a human face: a portrait for the why, a workshop moment for the what, and so on.
- End the arc cleanly. The final post should feel like a close, then the feed shifts to always-on.

## Repurposing within the launch

Each beat is one idea expressed four ways, never copy-pasted. Write the Substack version of the beat first where one exists, then extract to LinkedIn, then X, then Instagram, following the extraction order in `substack.md`.

## Guardrails specific to the launch

- Even in launch mode, every post carries substance. The arc front-loads positioning, but a beat still teaches, argues, or shows. No empty "we're open for business" posts.
- The direct CTA is reserved for the final beat. Earlier beats invite thought, not bookings.
- No motivational or inspirational register, even for the founding-idea post. It is conviction, not a TED talk.
- Industry-partner beats stay general; no named products or sponsors.
- Run every beat through the master checklist, including the em dash check, and offer a vibe-check before the arc goes live.
