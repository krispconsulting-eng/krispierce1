# LinkedIn (the core channel)

LinkedIn is where the consulting practice lives. It is where decision-makers in health organisations, industry, and the NFP sector actually read. If only one channel gets attention, it is this one. Treat every other platform as a feeder back to here.

## What LinkedIn is for

- Establishing Kris as a peer and an authority among people who commission this work.
- Demonstrating method and judgement, not announcing availability.
- Starting conversations in the comments that turn into enquiries offline.

## Post structure that works here

LinkedIn rewards a strong first line (the "hook line" shown before "see more") and short, scannable paragraphs.

1. **Hook line.** One sentence that earns the click. A specific observation, a sharp claim, or a number. Never "I'm excited to share" and never a question that sounds like an ad.
2. **The turn.** One or two short paragraphs that develop the single idea. Concrete before abstract. Name the real version versus the tokenistic version where it helps.
3. **The point.** What this means for the reader's work. This is where positioning happens, quietly.
4. **A light close.** An invitation to think or respond, not a hard CTA. "Curious whether others are seeing the same" beats "DM me to work together".

Keep it to one idea. If a second idea is fighting to get in, it is the next post.

## Format notes

- Length: roughly 120 to 250 words for a standard post. Long enough to make the argument, short enough to finish.
- Line breaks between most sentences or pairs of sentences. Dense blocks lose readers on mobile.
- No more than three to five hashtags, placed at the end, all relevant (for example consumer engagement, co-design, HTA, rare disease, health policy).
- One link per post belongs in the first comment, not the body, when reach matters. State in the post that the link is below.
- Plain language. The reader is senior and busy, not impressed by jargon.

## Profile copy

When writing or revising the profile, the spine drives it.

- **Headline:** what she does and who for, in her register. Lead with the outcome (patient-centred outcomes, insight generation) not the job title.
- **About section:** open with the one-line positioning, then the credibility points as evidence rather than a brag list, then who she helps and the angle for each, then a plain line on how to start a conversation.
- **Featured:** pin the strongest insight post or the launch announcement.

## Comment strategy

Comments are half the channel. They are where authority compounds without a single new post.

- Reply to every substantive comment on her own posts with another idea, not just thanks.
- Comment on the posts of people in HTA, genomics, rare disease, research, and the NFP sector. A sharp, generous comment on the right post is often worth more than a post of her own.
- Disagree well when she disagrees. A reasoned, evidence-backed counterpoint reads as expertise. Snark does not.

## Guardrails on LinkedIn

- No engagement-bait formatting (no "comment YES below", no fake line-break suspense with no payoff).
- No motivational register. She is talking to peers.
- Keep industry-partner content general; no named products or sponsors.
- Run the master checklist, including the em dash check, before posting.
