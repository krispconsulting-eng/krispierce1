# Substack (the long-form anchor)

Substack is where the full argument gets made. It anchors the whole system: write here first, then extract everything else. A strong Substack piece becomes a LinkedIn post, an X thread, and an Instagram caption without ever copy-pasting. It is also the asset that builds an owned audience Kris controls, unlike the platforms.

## What Substack is for

- Making the complete case for an idea, with room for nuance the other channels cannot hold.
- Building a subscriber list that belongs to Kris, not to an algorithm.
- Establishing depth: the place a prospective client goes to understand how she actually thinks.

## Why write here first

The long-form version forces the full argument. Once the whole thought exists, extraction is easy and honest: you are pulling the sharpest part out of a finished argument, not inflating a thin one. Drafting short first tends to produce posts that gesture at an idea they never actually made.

## Structure of a piece

1. **Title and subtitle.** The title earns the open in an inbox. The subtitle states the promise plainly.
2. **Open with the concrete.** A specific moment, finding, or tension from the work. Earn the reader's attention before any framing.
3. **The argument.** Develop one central idea across the piece. Sub-headings to keep it navigable. Real versus tokenistic, evidence, the systems angle from her HTA and genomics experience where it fits.
4. **The implication.** What this means for health organisations, industry, or NFPs, depending on who the piece is for. This is where positioning lives, earned by the argument that preceded it.
5. **Close.** A clear thought to leave with. A light invitation to subscribe or to reply, never a hard sell.

## Format notes

- Length: long enough to fully make one argument, usually 800 to 1500 words. Depth, not padding.
- One central idea per piece. Supporting points are fine; a second thesis is a second piece.
- Sub-headings and short paragraphs keep long-form readable.
- Cadence over volume: a genuinely good piece every two to four weeks beats a thin weekly.

## Extraction order (after the piece is written)

- **To LinkedIn:** take the single sharpest argument from the piece and make it fully as a standalone post. Not a summary.
- **To X:** find the one line that does the most work and build a short post or a tight thread around it.
- **To Instagram:** lead with the human or visual entry point that opened the piece, then carry one idea in the caption.

## Voice on Substack

The fullest expression of the register: expert, plain, willing to commit to a view and defend it. Room for nuance, none for waffle. Lived experience can ground the piece but does not carry it.

## Guardrails on Substack

- One central idea per piece.
- Keep industry-partner material general; no named products or sponsors, Medicines Australia Code applies.
- Run the master checklist, including the em dash check, before publishing.
